## Definition
Dormant reactivation is an account with little or no activity for a long period that suddenly becomes very active. Criminals buy or borrow dormant accounts because they have a clean history and older account dates, which lowers scrutiny. A reactivation followed by high volume is also a sign of account takeover, where a fraudster has gained control of a genuine customer's credentials.

## Red flags
- No customer-initiated activity for 180 days or more, followed by inflows above 4 times the previous average; above 10 times is a strong indicator.
- Reactivation followed within days by large transfers out, especially to new counterparties.
- New counterparties, new countries or a new channel compared with the account's history.
- Activity that does not fit the customer's segment or declared income.
- Changes to contact details or devices shortly before the reactivation (where available).

## Common benign explanations
- A customer returning from a long period abroad, on leave or studying, recorded in KYC.
- A retiree who uses the account only for occasional large payments.
- A salary account revived when the customer starts a new job, with the employer as the sender.
- Proceeds of a documented asset sale arriving into a rarely used account.

## What to check
1. The length of the dormant period and what, if anything, happened during it.
2. The size of the reactivation inflow compared with the account's earlier average.
3. Who the new counterparties are and where they are located.
4. Whether the activity after reactivation resembles the customer's earlier pattern or is new.
5. KYC notes for travel, leave, a new employer, or a recorded event.

## Evidence needed to close
- KYC records a reason for both the dormancy and the return (travel, leave, new job), quoted exactly.
- The new inflows come from a counterparty consistent with that reason (a new employer, the customer's own account abroad).
- The activity after reactivation resembles normal spending for the segment.
Cite the first reactivation transactions and the KYC wording.

## Evidence needed to escalate
Escalate when a dormant account receives large unexplained inflows, when funds leave quickly to new counterparties, or when the post-reactivation pattern bears no resemblance to the earlier account history. Cite the reactivation inflows and any onward transfers.
