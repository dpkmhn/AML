## Definition
A funnel account collects funds from many unrelated senders and pays them out to one or very few receivers. It concentrates money from many sources, often across a network of mules or victims, into a single destination. Funnel accounts appear in fraud proceeds collection, in scam networks and in informal value transfer.

## Red flags
- Ten or more distinct senders in 30 days, with outflows going to two or fewer counterparties.
- Senders with no evident relationship to the customer, or to each other, often sending similar amounts.
- Inflows from several emirates or countries arriving within a short period.
- Outflows that move most of the collected total in a few large transfers.
- A personal account showing business-like collection without a declared business.
- Senders' references that are generic, numbered ("payment 3"), or that mention investments, prizes or jobs.

## Common benign explanations
- A landlord collecting rent from many tenants, recorded in KYC with the number of units.
- A business owner whose customers pay by transfer and who settles with one main supplier.
- A group organiser collecting contributions (a family event, a savings circle) with a documented purpose.
- A sports club or community treasurer, where the role is recorded in KYC.

## What to check
1. The count of distinct senders in the window and in the baseline. Is the spread new?
2. The relationship between senders and the customer, and the amounts each sent.
3. Where the money went: how many receivers, how quickly, and whether any receiver is abroad.
4. The declared segment and expected activity: does it allow third-party collections?
5. KYC notes for properties let, a business, or an organising role.

## Evidence needed to close
- KYC records a reason to receive from many parties (rental units, a declared trade, an organising role), quoted exactly.
- The number of senders and the amounts fit that reason, for example monthly rent of similar size from a stable set of tenants.
- The receivers fit the reason too (a building management company, a supplier, the customer's own account).
Cite the sender transactions and the main outflows.

## Evidence needed to escalate
Escalate when many unrelated senders feed one or two receivers and KYC gives no reason, when senders change from month to month, or when the receivers are abroad or unknown. Cite a representative set of inbound transactions and every concentrated outflow.
