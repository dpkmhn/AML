## Definition
High-risk geography covers transfers to or from jurisdictions with weak AML controls, high levels of corruption or organised crime, or links to sanctioned activity. In this project the high-risk list is synthetic: country codes XA, XB and XC. In production the list would come from the FATF "black" and "grey" lists, the UAE National Risk Assessment and the bank's own country risk model. A single transfer to such a country is not suspicious by itself, but it raises the level of scrutiny for the rest of the account activity.

## Red flags
- Any transfer to or from a listed country; three or more in the window is a strong indicator.
- Transfers to a listed country with no family, business or nationality link recorded in KYC.
- Amounts that grow over time, or that are split into several transfers on the same day.
- Inflows from a listed country followed by outflows elsewhere (combining with pass-through).
- Counterparties that change frequently, or names that do not match the stated relationship.

## Common benign explanations
- Regular family support to relatives in the customer's home country, recorded in KYC with the relationship and expected amount.
- Payment for documented goods or services by a business whose declared trade involves that country.
- Tuition or medical payments to a named institution.

## What to check
1. The customer's nationality and any family or business links recorded in KYC.
2. Every transfer involving a listed country in the window and in the baseline: amounts, dates, counterparties.
3. Whether the amounts and frequency are stable (a monthly remittance) or rising and irregular.
4. Whether the same counterparty receives every time, and whether its name fits the stated relationship.
5. Any other typology signals on the account, since geography amplifies them.

## Evidence needed to close
- KYC records the relationship or business reason for transfers to that country, quoted exactly, with an expected amount or frequency.
- The transfers match that record: same counterparty, similar amounts, regular timing.
- No other typology signals in the window.
Cite each high-risk transfer and the KYC wording.

## Evidence needed to escalate
Escalate when transfers to or from a listed country have no recorded reason, when they are irregular or growing, when they combine with another typology signal, or when the customer is rated high risk or is a PEP. Cite every transfer involving a listed country.
