## Definition
A pass-through (or "transit") account receives funds and sends almost all of them on within hours or a day or two, so the account holder acts as a link in a chain rather than an end user of the money. It is a layering technique: each hop puts distance between the funds and their source. Money mules recruited through job or romance scams often run pass-through accounts without knowing the origin of the funds.

## Red flags
- 80% or more of an inflow sent out within 48 hours, repeated two or more times in 30 days.
- Inflows from counterparties with no visible link to the customer, followed by outflows to different counterparties.
- Balance returning to near zero after each cycle; little or no ordinary spending in between.
- Outflows split into several smaller transfers, or sent abroad.
- Activity that does not fit the declared profile, for example a student moving AED 60,000 a month.
- References such as "loan", "investment", "help" or "commission" with no supporting record.

## Common benign explanations
- Moving one's own savings between the customer's own accounts, for example to a brokerage or an account at another bank in the customer's name.
- Paying a known large expense from a known inflow: a car purchase funded by a family transfer, school fees paid from an annual bonus.
- A business owner receiving client payments and paying suppliers the same week, where the counterparties match the declared trade.

## What to check
1. Who sent the money and who received it. Do the names match the customer or known relationships?
2. The time between each inflow and its outflow, and the share of the inflow that left.
3. How many cycles occurred in the window, and whether this pattern appears in the baseline period.
4. Whether ordinary living spend (cards, utilities, rent) continues alongside the cycles.
5. KYC notes for a recorded own-account transfer, investment account or one-off purchase.

## Evidence needed to close
- The outgoing counterparty is the customer (same name, own account at another institution) or a payee explained in KYC notes, quoted exactly.
- The cycles are few and tied to a recorded event, not an ongoing pattern.
- The inflow source is consistent with the profile (salary, bonus, known family member).
Cite the inflow and outflow transaction ID pairs and the KYC wording.

## Evidence needed to escalate
Escalate when funds from unrelated senders leave to unrelated receivers within 48 hours and KYC records no reason, when cycles repeat through the window, or when outflows go to high-risk jurisdictions. Cite each inflow and outflow pair. A customer whose profile cannot support the volume (students, retirees) is a strong indicator of mule activity.
