## Definition
Structuring (also called smurfing) is splitting cash into several deposits so each one stays below the bank's cash reporting threshold. Falcon Digital Bank applies an internal cash threshold of AED 50,000 per deposit for this guidance. Structuring belongs to the placement stage: it is how illicit cash first enters the banking system without triggering a report. Structuring is suspicious in itself, even when the underlying funds later prove legitimate, because the intent is to avoid a control.

## Red flags
- Three or more cash deposits between 90% and 100% of the threshold (AED 45,000 to 49,999.99) within 14 days.
- Deposits on consecutive or near-consecutive days, often at similar times or through the same channel.
- Round or near-identical amounts, for example AED 48,500, AED 49,000 and AED 48,900.
- Cash activity that is new for the customer or out of line with the declared occupation.
- Deposits followed soon after by a transfer out of a similar total, which suggests the cash was only passing through.
- References that are blank, generic ("deposit", "cash") or inconsistent with each other.

## Common benign explanations
- A cash-intensive business (grocery, restaurant, salon, trading shop) banking daily or weekly takings. Takings vary with the day of the week and rarely cluster just under one figure.
- A customer selling a car or other asset for cash and depositing it in instalments for convenience, with a sale document on file.
- Seasonal trading peaks (Ramadan, Eid, Dubai Shopping Festival) for a declared retail business.

## What to check
1. The customer's segment, occupation and expected activity in KYC. Is cash expected at all?
2. The full list of cash deposits in the 90 days before the alert. Is the near-threshold band new, or has the customer always deposited at these levels?
3. The spread of amounts. Genuine takings vary; structured deposits cluster in a narrow band below the threshold.
4. What happened to the money afterwards: did it stay, fund normal spending, or leave quickly?
5. KYC notes for any recorded cash source (business takings, asset sale).

## Evidence needed to close
Close only when all of the following hold:
- KYC records a cash-generating occupation or a specific cash source, quoted exactly in the case.
- The deposit amounts and frequency are consistent with that source over the baseline period, not only in the alert window.
- The funds were not moved out to unrelated counterparties within days.
Cite the deposit transaction IDs and the KYC wording. A plausible story without a KYC record is not enough; route to Needs more info instead.

## Evidence needed to escalate
Escalate when the near-threshold deposits are unexplained by KYC, when the pattern is new compared with the baseline, or when the cash is followed by rapid outward transfers. Cite every deposit in the band and any linked outflow. A high-risk customer showing even a weak structuring pattern is escalated under the escalation criteria.
